Lesson 1, Video 1: Welcome/Approaches to Teaching with the AI Fluency Framework
If you're here, you've already been working with the AI Fluency Framework yourself. Maybe you've discovered something powerful in how it helps you think about AI collaboration. Now you're wondering: How do I share this with my students?
That’s exactly what this course is about. Whether you're teaching students or fellow educators, this course will equip you with pedagogical strategies, assessment approaches, and ways to effectively communicate the AI Fluency Framework to others.
Before we dive into the course, let's make sure we’re on the same page about the nature of the framework. The framework operates on two levels:
First, it describes what actually happens when people work with AI—the decisions, the communication, the evaluation, the responsibility. It maps the territory.
Second, it also guides people toward better practices. It doesn't just describe what people do; it shows them what they should do to work with AI effectively, efficiently, ethically, and safely.
This dual nature is crucial for teaching AI Fluency. Your students need to understand both how AI interactions work AND how to make those interactions better. The framework gives us a language and structure for both.
We’ve organized this course around three essential questions every educator faces when introducing AI Fluency:
In this lesson, we ask how can we introduce the framework to students? We’ll look at pedagogy and different ways of exploring the framework with your students.
In Lesson 2, we ask how do we know if students are getting it? We’ll look at Assessment and how to measure and evaluate AI Fluency.
Finally in Lesson 3 we ask how do we connect this to what we’re already teaching? We’ll look at Curriculum and how to integrate AI Fluency within a discipline or field.


For the rest of this video, we will look at pedagogy and different ways of exploring the framework with your students. In our experience, we have found it useful to share the AI Fluency Framework with students using four different approaches or entry points. These strategies each add conceptual richness and depth of detail to support learning for different groups and contexts. 
["Approach 1: Step-by-Step (Linear)"]
The most straightforward approach is to treat the Framework as a linear process, in which we move from Delegation to Description to Discernment to Diligence.  For students just starting to learn how to work with AI, this approach feels natural, manageable, and intuitive. It’s similar to how you might have first learned the framework yourself.
This waterfall-style approach helps students to build a basic process for their AI interactions, and to approach the competencies sequentially.
For example, you might structure a four-part assignment where part 1 focuses on Delegation and working through project planning, part 2 develops Description skills through hands on workshops where they interact directly with AI, part 3 builds Discernment through evaluating AI outputs together, and part 4 addresses Diligence through ethical discussions and transparency practices. Each part builds on the previous, creating a structured learning journey.
[ "Approach 2: Start Anywhere (Non-linear)"]
The second approach acknowledges that the 4Ds aren't actually a fixed sequence. Rather they're an interconnected system. This means you can start anywhere and bounce around as needed. For students with more AI experience, this approach adds real world complexity and flexibility. 
Consider these scenarios:
Sometimes the problems that we perceive through Discernment leads to entirely new Delegation decisions. For example, a student evaluating AI-generated code might realize the AI system they chose isn't suited for their programming paradigm, prompting them to reconsider their AI assistant selection.
Or, sometimes Diligence directly shapes your Description strategy. For example, when working on sensitive data, privacy requirements might dictate that students craft prompts without including personally identifiable information, fundamentally changing how they communicate with AI.
Or, sometimes Description challenges reveal gaps in Delegation. For example, a student struggling to articulate what they want from an AI partner might realize they haven't clearly defined their own project goals, sending them back to revisit fundamental Delegation questions.
This more holistic approach helps students understand AI Fluency as an adaptive, responsive practice rather than a rigid checklist, preparing them for the messy realities of actual AI collaboration.
This approach lends itself to group projects and case-based learning, presenting students with real AI collaboration scenarios and letting them discover which competencies are needed and when.
[ "Approach 3: Just One D (Focused)" (2 slides)]
The third approach involves deep explorations of just one competency at a time. This is a good approach for specialized purposes (like a discussion or skill building session). You can spend an entire workshop on just Description, exploring concrete techniques for all three sub-components with hands-on practice. Or you focus a discussion seminar entirely on Diligence.
For example, you could run three sessions on Discernment alone: one on evaluating AI-generated content for accuracy and utility (also known as Product Discernment), another on evaluating an AI's problem-solving approaches (Process Discernment), and a third on assessing whether an AI's communication style helps or hinders the collaboration process itself (Performance Discernment). This deep focus approach builds sophisticated evaluation skills relevant across many AI interactions.
This more focused approach helps students master a specific area, allowing for very nuanced exploration of techniques, edge cases, and disciplinary applications without the cognitive load of juggling all four Ds simultaneously.
[ Approach 4: Two Loops]
The fourth approach — understanding AI fluency as nested processes — represents the most conceptually-rich understanding of how AI Fluency can be applied in practice. Once students grasp the basics, this approach helps them think more deeply about fluent AI interaction.  
Here’s how this approach works. We can break human-AI interaction into two decision spaces:
The Delegation-Diligence loop handles the big picture: it involves strategic and ethical decision making. What am I trying to do? How should I divide work between myself and AI? Which systems align with my values? How do I ensure responsible collaboration?
The Description-Discernment loop handles the moment-to-moment decisions: it involves the tactical, iterative work of AI collaboration. How do I communicate this specific need? Is this output meeting my standards? How can I refine my approach?
In practice, these loops constantly inform each other. Strategic decisions shape tactical execution, and tactical discoveries reshape strategic thinking.
